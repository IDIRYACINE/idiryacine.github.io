*Measure cost per accepted piece of work. Usage is the number guaranteed not to track value.*

One reported case: a large engineering org rolled out a coding agent, adoption went from 32% to 84% of ~5,000 engineers, partly driven by an internal usage leaderboard.

The year's AI budget was reportedly gone in four months.

A usage leaderboard rewards token consumption, which is about the one number guaranteed not to track value.

Some figures worth knowing:

- Agentic tasks use roughly 5–30× the tokens of a chat query
- Ten correction cycles can cost ~50× a single clean pass
- Two runs of the same task can differ by up to 30× in tokens
- Spending more didn't reliably buy a better answer

And the correction loop, the most expensive part, is the dominant way people actually work.

The metric I'd use instead: cost per accepted piece of work.

Not spend per person. Not adoption rate. Not tokens.

How does your team measure whether AI spend is worth it?
