The seed decides the shuffling, the augmentation draws and the weight initialisation. On a dataset with a few hundred images, that's enough to swing accuracy by more than a point or two. A single run per model is a coin flip dressed up as a benchmark.

How I handle it:

1. Five seeds per configuration (42, 123, 456, 789, 1337). They seed every generator (PyTorch, CUDA, NumPy, Python, the data loader and each loader worker) and are re-applied every epoch, so shuffling, augmentation and initialisation are all functions of the seed.
2. Pair by seed. Model A at seed 42 is compared with model B at seed 42, on the same data order and the same augmentations. Shared noise cancels out of the difference, which makes a paired test far more sensitive than comparing two means.
3. Report the spread, not just the mean: mean ± SD over seeds, plus how many seeds each difference wins.
4. Be honest about small n. With three seeds, a 95% t-interval multiplies the standard error by 4.30. So at n = 3, my rules use a sign count (3 of 3) plus a minimum mean effect, not a p-value.
5. Watch the variance itself. In one experiment, dynamic convolution doubled the seed spread without improving the mean. You only see that if you run seeds.
