*One use case: a contract that declares everything a transport needs, one handler over ports, and bindings generated for both backends.*

1. The contract (on the image): input, output, route, plus three pieces of metadata.
- access: permit(itemsPermissions.create, …), so who may call it
- idempotent: true, so retries are safe
- writes: [itemsResource], so which cached data it makes stale

2. The handler, written once:
- the creator comes from the verified caller, never from client input
- the write goes through a port: itemsWriter(ctx).insert(…)
- the rest of the organisation gets notified

3. Everything else is generated: a NestJS route with its transaction and error mapping, a Convex mutation, the typed client, the cache invalidation on every frontend.

What I don't write: a controller, a DTO, an access check, a cache key, a second implementation for the other backend.

When the contract carries the facts, the plumbing can be derived.
