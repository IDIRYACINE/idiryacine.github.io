*AI made checking code expensive. Experienced developers, measured: what you pay for vs what you keep.*

In a measured study of experienced developers, about 39% of AI generations were accepted — and many of those still needed rework.

But you pay to read and judge 100% of them.

That's the real cost structure of AI-assisted work:

- Generation: nearly free
- Evaluation: paid on everything
- Benefit: collected on a minority

So the useful question isn't "how do I get the model to write more?"

It's "how do I shrink what I have to verify?"

Tighter scope. Smaller units. Constraints stated before the work starts, not discovered after.

I spent the last few months going through the research on how developers actually work with coding agents. This was the first finding that changed how I use the tools.

Where does most of your AI time go — writing prompts, or reading output?

## Sources

- METR, "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced OSS Developer Productivity" ([arXiv 2507.09089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089)). The 39% acceptance figure comes from secondary analyses of the paper (Simon Willison's notes).
