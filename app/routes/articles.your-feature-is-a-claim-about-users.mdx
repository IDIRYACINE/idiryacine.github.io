*Your feature is a claim about your users. Six ways a team can collaborate through AI. Each assumes teams are bad at…*

I mapped 26 ways a group of people can collaborate through AI. They fall into six families, each defined by what gets pooled:

- Shared knowledge → assumes teams are bad at remembering
- Shared reasoning → bad at explaining themselves
- Shared live session → bad at being in a room together
- Shared authority → bad at saying who's responsible
- Shared disagreement → bad at disagreeing productively
- Shared async context → bad at waiting

Every product picks one of these, usually without saying so.

Here's what surprised me:

Most products have attempted the first family, shared memory.

That's not because forgetting is the biggest problem. It's because storage is the easiest thing to build.

Meanwhile the families with the strongest published evidence, around disagreement and deliberation, have almost no products at all.

Pick the wrong assumption and you get something technically excellent that nobody uses.

Before you build, write down the sentence: "Our users are bad at ___."

Then go check if it's true.

What would you fill in for your product?
