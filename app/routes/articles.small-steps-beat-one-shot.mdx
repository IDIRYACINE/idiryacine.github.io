*Small steps beat one-shot delegation. Same developers, same tool, same repos. Success on real issues.*

83% success when they worked with the agent step by step.

38% when they handed the whole task over at once.

That was the largest effect I found anywhere in the research on coding agents. And it has nothing to do with which model you use.

Why it works is the interesting part.

The main blocker was project-specific, tacit knowledge, and participants often couldn't put it into words, even when asked directly.

You can't write a spec for knowledge you can't articulate.

But you can answer one small question at a time, as each decision comes up.

Small steps turn knowledge you can't articulate into a series of questions you can answer.

They also cap the damage. A small step that goes wrong costs a small re-review. A one-shot task that goes wrong costs a full one.

Caveat: developers chose their own strategy, so careful developers may simply be better developers. Trust the direction more than the exact number.

Do you hand agents whole tasks, or slices?
