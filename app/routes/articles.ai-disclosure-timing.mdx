*AI disclosure: timing is everything. Randomised field experiment on real transactions. Conversion rate.*

A randomised field experiment on real transactions:

- AI revealed before the conversation → conversion fell from 23.7% to 4.8%
- AI revealed after the decision → 23.2%, basically no change

The fact that AI was involved barely mattered. The timing carried almost all of the effect.

Other findings point the same way:

- Saying "AI was used" lowered patient trust, but saying it along with who checked the work didn't
- Most people want to be told: 58% of patients, and 84–91% of consumers want AI content labelled

So silence isn't safe, and disclosing up front isn't free.

What seems to work is disclosure plus accountability: "AI helped draft this, and here's who checked it." Ideally that's a team rule, not a judgement call made fresh every time.

Because when it goes wrong, the person who signed it is the one held responsible, not the AI vendor.

Does your team have a rule for when to say AI was involved?
