*At one rate per model, a ranking can be an artefact of the rate. Reporting every model at both rates shows which conclusions survive.*

Small CNNs trained on a few hundred images are very sensitive to the learning rate. Train two architectures at one rate each, and the difference you measure can be the rate rather than the model.

So in my comparisons:

- Every model, baselines included, is trained at two learning rates, and both results appear in every table.
- The rate with the higher mean validation score is starred and quoted by default. The choice is made on validation, never on test.
- When a conclusion would change at the other rate, I say so.

For external baselines I go further: each one is run ImageNet-pretrained and from scratch over a learning-rate grid, and reported at its own best rate. And I'm explicit that this is a fair benchmark, not a ceiling.

It cuts both ways. When one of my designs beat a randomly initialised stem on retinal grading, the first thing I checked was a second rate. The gap held at both, so it wasn't a learning-rate artefact. On breast ultrasound, every gap was inside the noise at both rates, and I reported it as such.

And "no extra head beats the bare trunk" is a claim I only make because it held at both rates on every dataset.

It doubles the compute. It also removes the most common way a comparison quietly goes wrong.
