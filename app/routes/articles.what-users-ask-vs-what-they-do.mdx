*What users ask for vs what they do. A branching AI chat tool, five-day field study, 24 people.*

In a five-day study of a branching AI chat tool, participants said beforehand that they wanted to save and reuse good prompts.

So the tool shipped prompt templates.

Usage over five days: 18 times in total, by 5 of 24 people.

Meanwhile the summaries and tags the tool generated automatically got used constantly.

Two lessons:

1. A feature request is a guess about behaviour, not evidence of it.
2. Structure that appears for free gets used. Structure people have to stop and create doesn't. Nobody wants to pay a setup cost in the middle of their flow.

The coding-agent data shows the same gap at scale. Professionals say they "plan everything and validate every output." Across 11,000+ sessions, the median was three messages.

That's why the best discovery question isn't "Would you use this?"

It's "Tell me about the last time this happened."

What people actually did tells you more than what they say they'd do.

What feature have you built because users asked, and then watched them ignore?
