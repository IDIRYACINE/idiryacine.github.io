*Declare once → derive → verify. Every fact has one home. Everything else is imported, generated, or checked.*

Declare once → derive everything → verify mechanically.

Every fact gets exactly one home: an entity, a permission, an error code, a use case.

Everything that depends on that fact is either imported from there or generated from it.

And every seam between layers has a machine check: types, lint rules, a shared test suite.

It sounds abstract until you see what it kills:

- The entity defined in five places
- The permission check that differs between server and UI
- The error message that says one thing on one backend and another on the other
- The cache invalidation someone forgot to update

Most of the strict rules in the repo exist to protect that one sentence. When a rule feels annoying, it usually turns out to be guarding it.

In the next posts I'll go through the patterns that came out of it, one at a time.
