PyTorch gives you a trade-off:

- cudnn.deterministic = True: same seed, same bits, every run. Slower.
- cudnn.benchmark = True: cuDNN times several convolution algorithms and picks the fastest. Faster, but the pick can vary between runs, so two runs at the same seed can differ in the low bits.

I'd started with full determinism. Then I asked what my results actually claim.

They never claim single-seed reproducibility. Every number is a mean ± standard deviation over several seeds, and that seed spread is around ±1.6 accuracy points. The cuDNN algorithm jitter is floating-point noise, around 1e-6, about a million times smaller. It can't move a reported number.

So determinism was buying a property my experiments don't use, at a real cost in training time. I switched autotuning on, and wrote the reasoning into the code next to the flag, including how to switch it back if a future use needs bit-exact runs.

What I kept is the determinism that does matter:

- Data order, augmentation and dropout stay seeded, re-applied each epoch
- Each data-loader worker is seeded from the main seed. Without that, worker RNG comes from the OS, and data order varies between runs even with a fixed seed. That bug is easy to miss.

Rigour isn't maximising every guarantee. It's knowing which guarantee your claim depends on, and protecting that one.

Do you know which kind of reproducibility your results need?
