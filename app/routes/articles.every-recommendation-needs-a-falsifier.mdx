*Every recommendation needs a falsifier. The line most strategy decks leave out.*

For example:

- Recommendation: make units of work small.
- Wrong if: the success gap between small and large tasks is a selection effect, not a real effect.

Each conclusion also gets a confidence mark:

- Well evidenced: several independent sources
- Directional: one good study, with its weakness named
- Ruled out: tested and failed

And the brief ends with a section called "What we still don't know."

Writing it this way changed three things:

1. It stops you treating a bet like a fact. No study measured whether our product idea works, so every recommendation was a bet with a stated basis.
2. It tells you what to test first. The cheapest experiment is the one that would prove your favourite recommendation wrong.
3. It makes the doc honest about its own blind spots. Ours admitted we had measured developers but never the buyers, and the two groups barely overlap.

A recommendation you can't prove wrong isn't really a recommendation. It's an opinion.
