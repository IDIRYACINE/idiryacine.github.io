*Keep a graveyard of rejected ideas. Every decision keeps its reasoning next to it.*

Our product proposals live in three places:

- Under review
- Accepted
- Rejected

Every rejected proposal gets its own folder, with a short note next to it: what was decided, who decided it, and which criteria it failed.

A few lines are enough.

Why keep the dead ones?

Because three weeks later, someone will pitch the same idea with a new name and fresh excitement. Without the record, you argue the whole thing again from scratch.

With it, you open the folder:

"We looked at this. It failed on X. Has X changed?"

Sometimes the answer is yes, and that's a great conversation.

Usually it's no, and you just saved a week.

The record of why you said no is often worth more than the idea itself.

Do you keep your rejected ideas, or do they just disappear?
