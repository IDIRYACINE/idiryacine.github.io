*Write the decision rules and what each outcome means before the runs start. Then hold yourself to them.*

For one decisive round of experiments, the rules were fixed in my notes before any run started:

1. Base effect: the new first stage must beat normal training on every seed, per dataset.
2. The data matters: data-fitted filters must beat random and fixed-basis filters by at least one AUC point on average, on every seed, on most datasets.
3. Stacking: does a second level pay, under the same thresholds?
4. Learning rate: the gain counts as a rate artefact if a slower baseline comes within one point.

And the meaning of each branch, also written in advance:

- Rule 2 passes: data-fitted filters are the contribution
- Rule 2 fails and rule 1 passes: the data-fitted idea is closed, and what stands is the fixed stage itself
- Either way, this is the last round on this idea. What follows is writing it up.

Two more safeguards:

- Any per-dataset choice, like whether to keep brightness, is made on validation, never on test
- With three seeds, the t multiplier is 4.30, so the operative test is the sign count plus a mean threshold, not a p-value

Rule 2 failed. So the claim changed, and I reframed the whole piece of work around what actually held.

Without the rules, it would have been very tempting to run one more variant until the fitting looked useful.

Do you decide what counts as success before or after you see the results?
