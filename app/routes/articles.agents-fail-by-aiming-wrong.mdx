*Rejected AI PRs: aimed wrong, not written wrong. 306 rejected agent PRs, hand-labelled twice.*

Researchers hand-labelled 306 rejected agent PRs, twice and independently:

- Rejected as an incorrect fix: 5.6%
- Rejected because the work stopped mattering — abandoned, superseded, deprioritised: 24.5%

Relevance failures outnumber implementation failures 2.5 to 1.

The biggest single reason was that nobody carried the work to done (17.3%).

Once asking an agent for something costs less than caring about the result, you get a lot of work that nobody follows through on.

So a better model attacks the ~10% slice of failures.

Better aim attacks the bigger one: was this worth starting, and who owns finishing it?

Honest caveat: about half the sample had no recorded reason for rejection. If those turn out to be mostly bad code, the ratio narrows.

Are your agent tasks failing on quality, or on aim?
