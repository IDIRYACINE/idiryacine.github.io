*Settle positions before you ask the AI. Deliberation patterns with real evidence behind them.*

The model's answer becomes the group's position before anyone has examined it. Nobody wants to be the person arguing with it in front of everyone. And a summary tuned for approval can quietly drop the one objection that should have stopped the decision.

Two of the best-evidenced ideas I found come from civic deliberation, not tech:

1. Form positions privately first

In the Habermas Machine study (5,734 participants), everyone wrote down their view alone. Then the AI drafted one shared statement for the group to scrutinise.

People rated those statements clearer and less biased than ones written by human mediators, and the successful statements kept minority critiques in.

2. Remove the loudest affordance

Polis removed the reply button. People can only agree, disagree or pass. That suppressed posturing, and it has been used in national policy processes in Taiwan.

Neither has really made it into workplace tools yet.

The lesson for teams: settle your positions before you ask the AI, not after.
